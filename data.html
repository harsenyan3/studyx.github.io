Research Evidence

Methodology, Findings, and Analysis

This page documents the usability data behind the redesign recommendations.

Methodology

To test Studyx.ai, we reached out to 6 participants, all of whom were either pursuing higher education or were in an educational field. Of the 6 participants, 3 were undergraduate college students pursuing different studies, including Chemistry, Industrial Architecture, and Landscape Architecture. The remaining participants consisted of a Graduate Student Studying Computer Science, a high school student taking AP-level courses, and a professor. We gathered a group with varying levels of education to observe how different types of users interacted with StudyX beyond undergraduate college students.

Our usability testing consisted of a pre-test questionnaire, a moderated interview, and a post-test questionnaire. For the moderated interview, we drafted 7 different tasks for users to attempt while using StudyX.ai. These tasks ranged from asking the user to look for onboarding tools to uploading notes to generate summaries to using the AI-generated quiz feature and reviewing its feedback. The goal behind these tasks was to observe how users interact with the various parts of StudyX.ai and how well users can navigate the site with minimal instruction. These tasks served as the basis of our moderated interview.

Before conducting our interview, we gave participants the pre-test questionnaire to gather information about their current academic level, record their study habits, and gauge their usual usage of AI. In the interview, the participant shared their screen on a virtual call with one of our team members while the tester recorded the call. During the interview, the tester provided the participant with a task and observed how the user interacted with StudyX.AI, asking them to discuss their thought process as they worked with the app. Once the user completed their task or found themselves unable to do so due to the application's limitations, we gave the participant another task, continuing the process until completing our list of tasks. After completing the list of tasks, we asked the participants to complete the post-test questionnaire, which included questions about their general thoughts regarding StudyX.ai, features they most appreciated, and any adjustments they would like to see.

To best find which aspects of StudyX needed the most adjustments, we recorded which tasks users commonly struggled with or were unable to complete during the interview. Furthermore, we analyzed the post-test questionnaire responses, compiling responses regarding specific functionalities and grouping common themes among their answers. By observing these patterns in user responses, we were able to interpolate which features users had issues with and which they found useful.

Findings

Finding 1: The landing page and navigation did not clearly guide users

Four of the five participants who responded to our post-test questionnaire described the landing page as difficult to understand due to a lack of onboarding tools. One response noted that the site was very purple, and the design made it difficult to know where to start. Another response noted that while the UI was very clean, its overall design was not intuitive, making it difficult to navigate. One user also responded, stating that the sign-up and payment elements on the site were overbearing and at times intrusive, which became very distracting.

Finding 2: Upload, processing, and organization created uncertainty

Five of the six participants had issues uploading documents during the user testing session, and four participants mentioned issues with upload speeds and organization in their post-test questionnaire. During the testing session, one user wanted to upload multiple files at once, which StudyX.AI was unable to accommodate without using up many of their tokens. Another user mentioned that other, less specialized AI tools, such as ChatGPT, performed many of the functions StudyX could, but significantly faster and with more flexibility. Users also commonly had to refresh the page while uploading documents, which not only consumed their tokens but required them to upload their file again.

Finding 3: Quiz generation was the strongest existing feature, but feedback was inconsistent

The most appreciated feature of StudyX.ai, according to our user testing, was the quiz generation feature. Four of the five post-test questionnaires found the quiz generation tool useful and the tool they would most likely return to. However, when asked to rate the AI quiz feedback, scores varied widely, spanning from 3 to 8 out of 10, with an average of 5.2. This showed that quiz feedback varied widely in quality and needed a redesign.

Finding 4: AI assistance needed to be easier to find and more conversational

All participants who completed the post-test questionnaire commented that the AI assistance feature was difficult to access and locate. One user noted that the AI chatbot's response was incorrect, while another was completely unable to access the assistant due to a lack of credits after one session. Another user noted that having the AI chatbot only accessible after initially uploading a file was clunky. This feedback indicated that the AI chat feature needed a full redesign with a more prominent role in the overall application's design.

Finding 5: The visual design and organization was confusing for users

Many users commented that StudyX.ai was poorly organized and found it difficult to navigate the multitude of features. Many users struggled to find specific features due to the poorly organized sidebar and spent a great deal of time finding certain features they needed for a task. This indicated the need for a formatting and palette change to create a more organized interface.

Analysis

As Figure 1 shows, four out of the five users found the landing page and overall navigation confusing, due to a lack of onboarding tools present on the website. Furthermore, one participant quoted that the landing page was very purple and it was difficult to know where to start, as seen in Table 1. Given this, we knew that one of the most important parts of our redesign had to be a complete overhaul of the landing page to provide a more user-friendly experience. To do so, we elected to simplify the landing page so users are less overwhelmed as they open the home page. Additionally, we added more descriptive buttons that describe each feature's function and purpose to help with new user onboarding.

Our findings also indicated a need to overhaul the AI assistance feature to be more user-oriented. As shown in Figure 1, all users in our user testing who responded to the post-test questionnaire had major issues with the implementation of the AI assistance. This clearly displays a need to adjust the current system. To do so in our redesign, we expanded the AI Tutor's functionality, allowing it to hold full conversations with users based on their prompts, similar to the average chatbot. This redesign removes the old, token-intensive system and instead allows users to freely speak to the chatbot.

Another important note from our findings was users' problems with StudyX.ai's organizational methods, or lack thereof. As Figure 2 shows, half of our users had concerns with the organization of elements on StudyX.ai, with many noting in their post-test questionnaire that the poor structure made it difficult to find certain features. From this, we concluded that another major part of our redesign was to reorganize the page content for easier reading. This led us to add more headers and block off specific sections of the app more clearly to help organize the content into more readable blocks. A major aspect of our redesign is that the sidebar menu removes many unnecessary features and instead replaces them with a smaller section of AI tools, separating it from another section for accessibility features. This allows users to know what each button does more quickly and makes navigation much simpler.

Another major finding from our user research was users' issues with the uploading and saving process. As Figure 1 shows, nearly every user, four out of five, had issues with the upload process, and half of them had issues with the library feature. Additionally, Figure 2 shows that two of our users noted problems with the upload interactions and slow processing speed. This indicated a need to improve the feedback given when uploading a file onto the platform. As such, we opted to add progress bars and other confirmation messages to our redesign to help users track the state of their uploads.

Our findings also indicated that quizzes were the most appreciated feature. As shown in Figure 4, every response given in the post-test questionnaires indicated that participants would return to Quiz Generation if they were to use StudyX.ai again. However, one of the major issues with quiz generation was the feedback on the quiz questions. As Figure 3 shows, participants were split on how beneficial the quiz feedback was, with some users ranking it as high as 8 out of 10 and others as low as 3 out of 10. This indicated that rather than overhauling the quiz feature in our redesign, as that seemed to be well-received already, we would need to overhaul the AI feedback. This is what led us to focus on improving the AI Tutor, as it serves as the basis for the quiz AI feedback, so improving the AI Tutor would naturally improve quiz AI feedback.
