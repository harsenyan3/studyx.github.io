Project Redesign Group 6

Rethinking StudyX.ai

This is our redesign proposal for this AI-powered study platform.

Authors: Hayk Arsenyan, Lily Carras, Kenneth Doan, Hannah Rajan, Eduardo Valverde

Summary

This report details the usability study and redesign process of StudyX.ai. StudyX.ai is an AI-powered study tool that accepts uploaded documents and uses them to generate notecards, summaries, and quizzes, giving students additional resources to help them study. While this site has the potential to greatly benefit many academics by providing a much quicker way to create study resources, it is held back by poor, confusing, and clunky UI.

To design potential improvements for StudyX.ai, we reached out to 6 participants within varying levels of academia to help us find the core issues with StudyX.ai and discover which features needed the most changes. During said testing, we discovered that the main issues users had with StudyX.ai included poor onboarding tools, a lack of organization, missing feedback for file uploads, and a difficult-to-access AI Tutor with limited functionality.

We then created a high-fidelity redesign prototype using Figma to showcase possible changes the StudyX.ai developers could employ to improve their site. Some of these changes included simplifying the landing page, including more onboarding information, adding feedback for user input, such as progress bars and confirmation text, a simpler, more condensed sidebar to help users navigate through the site more easily, and converting the AI Tutor system to a more modern AI chatbot.

The key takeaway from this design process was an emphasis on quality over quantity. While StudyX.ai boasted a great number of features and tools to help students study, the overabundance of tools made the UI feel cluttered and hindered students, as the clutter made users unable to find the tools they needed. By redesigning the product into a much simpler version with more compact elements and better organization, we were able to create a UI that felt much more natural to navigate and improve the application's overall usability without the need for a multitude of features.

Introduction

StudyX.ai is an AI-integrated web application built to help students study utilizing AI-powered tools. StudyX.ai's main features are accepting document uploads and scanning them to create summaries, flashcards, notes, and quizzes to aid students in their education. As a study app, StudyX.ai is primarily targeted at students, with options to cater to several levels of education from elementary to graduate.

We selected to overhaul StudyX.ai as we believed it had great potential that could rival similar study apps on the market, like Quizlet, with its AI features automatically generating assets for students, making studying even easier. StudyX.ai offers multiple features that could help students quickly format in-class resources. However, with its current design, most of that potential is wasted due to poor organization, a difficult-to-navigate UI, and an overabundance of features that essentially perform the same task. We believed that with a full redesign, StudyX.ai could become a much easier-to-use study tool that could greatly benefit students.

For this project, we focused on catering to the target demographic of academically active students. For our usability study, we reached out to participants from multiple colleges, including Georgia Tech, Kennesaw State University, and the University of Georgia. In addition, we also reached out to participants of multiple levels of education, with a focus on undergraduate students. This was to cover the broad academic spectrum that StudyX.ai caters to and allow us to gather multiple different viewpoints on the application.

Going into our analysis of StudyX.ai, our initial hypothesis was that first-time users would struggle with the application due to the lack of onboarding features. Additionally, we expected the response quality from the AI to be poor, including incorrect information and AI hallucinations that may hinder users' ability to study. Furthermore, we expected the application would run very slowly due to the expansive sidebar and the overabundance of different AI tools.
